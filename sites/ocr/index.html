How your images stay private

  • No uploadThe text reader runs inside this tab. Your image goes from your disk or clipboard into your browser's memory, and nowhere else.
  • Other sites are blockedThe security policy (connect-src 'self') only lets this page load its reader and language files from this site. There's no server code here to receive an image.
  • Nothing is keptYour image and its text aren't saved anywhere. Close the tab and they're gone. Only your light/dark choice is remembered.
  • No analytics or cookiesNo tracking scripts, no ads, no account. Cloudflare, our host, keeps standard access logs like any web host. Your images and text aren't in them.

Read every line on GitHub. Reading is done by Tesseract through Tesseract.js, served from this domain.

Questions

What kind of images work best?

Screenshots and flat scans of printed text work best. For photos, hold the camera straight over the page, use good light, and get close enough that the text is sharp. Small, blurry, curved or very tilted text comes out with more mistakes.

Can it read handwriting?

Not well. The reader is trained on printed text, so neat block capitals sometimes work, but cursive and quick notes usually come out garbled.

Why is the first one slower?

The first time, your browser downloads the reading engine and the English language file from this site, up to about 6 MB. After that, an image usually takes a few seconds at most, a bit longer on older phones. Spanish, French, German and Portuguese each add a 0.6 to 1.1 MB file, only when you pick them.

Does it keep tables, columns and formatting?

No. You get plain text with the line breaks it saw. Bold, fonts and colors are dropped, and text in side-by-side columns or tables can get mixed together, so check those. Tick "Join lines into paragraphs" if you want sentences on one line.

Can it read a PDF or an iPhone HEIC photo?

Not a PDF. Take a screenshot of the page and drop that in instead. HEIC photos work in recent Safari on iPhone and Mac. Other browsers can't open them, so turn them into JPGs first (heic.lol, listed below, does that in your browser too).

Does it work on my phone, or offline?

Yes, in any recent phone browser. Very large photos are scaled down to about 16 megapixels so your phone doesn't run out of memory. Once the reader has loaded, you can go offline and keep reading images in the same tab, in any language you've already used there.
